# 06. Basic Link Building

**Level:** Intermediate | **Reading time:** 15 minutes | **Prerequisite:** 01-basic/04

## Goal
Understand how to earn healthy links and avoid risky ones.

## Principle
A link from another site works as a recommendation. Quality and relevance matter more than quantity.

## Healthy approaches
- Create resources worth referencing: data, research, tools, complete guides.
- Digital PR: stories or data worth media coverage.
- Partnerships and community: mentions from organizations genuinely related to you.
- Guest posts whose content is truly useful to the host site's readers.
- Fixing broken links that point to your site (reclamation).

## Link attributes
Google provides `rel="sponsored"` for paid links or ads, `rel="ugc"` for user-generated content, and `rel="nofollow"` for links you do not want to endorse. Links placed to manipulate ranking violate Google's spam policy (link spam), including link exchanges and purchases that pass ranking signals.

## What to avoid
- Buying links without the correct attributes.
- Site networks or satellite blogs whose only purpose is to pass links.
- Mass link exchanges.
- Repeated exact-match keyword anchor text in an unnatural way.
- Buying fake "mentions" on forums or cheap AI citation services.

## SpamBrain
This Google system can neutralize manipulative links without notice. The symptom (a heuristic, not a definite diagnosis): the backlink count in third-party tools goes up but organic clicks do not move.

## Measuring
Track new referring domains, their relevance, and whether real referral traffic arrives. Scores like DR or DA are third-party tool metrics, not Google metrics.

## Exercise
1. Find 10 sites in your niche worth contacting. Write why each is relevant.
2. Design one asset worth linking to (for example, results of a small survey you actually run).

## Comprehension check
- When is rel="sponsored" used?
- Why is DR not a Google metric?
