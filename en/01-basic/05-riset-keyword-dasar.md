# 05. Basic Keyword Research

**Level:** Basic | **Reading time:** 15 minutes | **Prerequisite:** modules 02 and 04

## Goal
Produce a starting keyword list for one page in a way you can defend.

## Steps
1. **Collect seeds.** From your product or service, customer questions, competitors, and Google Autocomplete.
2. **Expand.** Use Autocomplete, People Also Ask, "related searches", and the Queries report in Google Search Console if the site already has data.
3. **Determine intent.** Type the keyword into Google and look at the kinds of pages that appear. The page types in the SERP are the strongest hint of intent.
4. **Choose one primary keyword per page.** Plus a few secondary ones with the same meaning.
5. **Check feasibility.** Can you honestly make a page more useful than what already exists?

## One page, one purpose rule
Two pages chasing the same query will compete with each other (cannibalization). One strong page is better than two half-finished pages.

## About volume and difficulty numbers
Research tools give estimates. If you have no access to the data, write "N/A" in that column. Do not invent search volume, CPC, or position just to make a table look complete.

## Minimal mapping format
| Cluster | Primary keyword | Intent | Funnel stage | Target URL | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| example | example keyword | informational | TOFU | /example/ | needs creating |

A ready-to-use CSV template is in `templates/keyword-intent-map.csv`.

## Exercise
1. Pick one service. Collect 20 keywords from Autocomplete and People Also Ask.
2. Group them into 3 to 5 clusters based on intent, not word similarity.
3. Choose one primary keyword per cluster.

## Comprehension check
- Why is looking at the SERP more reliable than guessing intent from words alone?
- What do you write in the volume column if you have no data?
