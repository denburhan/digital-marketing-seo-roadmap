# 02. E-E-A-T and Trust

**Level:** Advanced | **Reading time:** 15 minutes | **Prerequisite:** 03-advanced/01

## Goal
Translate E-E-A-T into real work on the site.

## A clarification
E-E-A-T comes from Google's quality rater guidelines. It is not a single score you can see or optimize directly with one trick. Google states that its ranking systems use many signals to prioritize helpful, trustworthy content. For practitioners, E-E-A-T works as a way of thinking.

## Four letters, four jobs
- **Experience (first-hand):** original photos, work process, test results, stories that can be verified. A sentence like "in my opinion X matters" with no evidence is only opinion. What has value: real actions, result data, and direct observation.
- **Expertise:** authors with relevant background. YMYL topics (health, finance, law) need extra attention to qualifications and accuracy.
- **Authoritativeness:** others reference and acknowledge you. Built over time through quality and organic coverage.
- **Trust:** the most important. Clear contact, privacy policy, business transparency, site security, no false claims.

## Trust checklist
- [ ] Business identity and contact are clear
- [ ] Author and update date are visible
- [ ] Sources for claims are named
- [ ] No fabricated testimonials or numbers
- [ ] HTTPS and a secure site
- [ ] Correction policy: if there is a mistake, it is fixed and recorded

## Non-commodity content
The main differentiators: original data, real case studies, your own photos, test results. These are hard for machines to copy and hard to fake without risk.

## Exercise
1. Audit one page with the trust checklist. Mark pass or fail per item.
2. Write one case study page from real work, complete with data you can show.

## Comprehension check
- Why is E-E-A-T not a "single ranking factor"?
- What is the difference between experience and opinion?
